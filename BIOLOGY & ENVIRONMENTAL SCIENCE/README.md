### 2. Project Overview

This capstone project focuses on studying the Bhadla Solar Park in Rajasthan and the Pavagada Solar Park in Karnataka. These are two of India's major large-scale solar power projects.

The project explores how solar energy generation can be integrated with agricultural activities through **agrivoltaics**, a sustainable land-use approach that allows solar panels and crop cultivation to coexist on the same land.

The study examines the technical, environmental, and socio-economic aspects of both solar parks and identifies opportunities to improve land productivity, conserve water, protect biodiversity, and support rural communities.

### 3. Problem Statement

Large-scale solar parks require extensive areas of land, which can create conflicts between renewable energy development and agricultural activities.

The major challenges include:

- Competition between solar energy infrastructure and agricultural land.
- Reduced opportunities for traditional farming and grazing.
- Environmental impacts on soil quality and biodiversity.
- Underutilization of land beneath solar panels.
- Water management and maintenance difficulties.
- Socio-economic challenges faced by local farming communities.

This project investigates agrivoltaics as a potential solution to these challenges.

The study examines its large-scale solar infrastructure, semi-arid climatic conditions, land-use challenges, and opportunities for integrating agricultural activities.

The study examines its land-leasing model, community participation, renewable energy development, and possibilities for sustainable agricultural integration.

### 8. Expected Outcomes

The project highlights the potential benefits of agrivoltaic integration:

- Improved land-use efficiency.
- Simultaneous agricultural and electricity production.
- Better soil moisture conservation.
- Reduced water evaporation in suitable crop systems.
- Additional income opportunities for farmers.
- Improved rural employment opportunities.
- Sustainable renewable energy development.
- Better alignment with environmental sustainability goals.

**Note:** The project is a research-based comparative study. The proposed agrivoltaic improvements are recommendations and should not be interpreted as proof that full-scale agrivoltaic systems are already operational throughout both solar parks.
