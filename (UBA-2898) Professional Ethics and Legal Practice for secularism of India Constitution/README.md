## 1. Project Overview

The **Aberdeen Three Case** is an important case study in engineering ethics, environmental responsibility, hazardous waste management, and professional accountability.

The case concerns three civilian chemical engineers—Carl Gepp, David Freeman, and William Dee—who were associated with hazardous chemical management at the U.S. Army's Aberdeen Proving Ground in Maryland during the 1980s.

The incident highlights serious concerns about unsafe chemical storage, improper labeling, inadequate ventilation, and hazardous waste management. It demonstrates how negligence and failure to follow safety regulations can create risks to human health and the environment.

This project examines the ethical responsibilities of engineers, the importance of reporting unsafe practices, legal accountability, and the need for whistleblower protection in professional environments.

## 2. Problem Statement

Engineering professionals are responsible for ensuring public safety, environmental protection, and compliance with legal regulations. However, negligence, poor supervision, and failure to report unsafe working conditions can result in serious consequences.

The Aberdeen Three Case highlights the importance of professional accountability and ethical decision-making when hazardous materials are involved.

The main problem addressed in this project is the failure to maintain proper safety standards and the importance of taking responsible action when professional and environmental risks are identified.

## 4. Case Study Background

The Aberdeen Proving Ground is a U.S. Army research and testing facility located in Maryland, United States.

During the 1980s, concerns arose regarding the handling, storage, and disposal of hazardous chemicals at the facility. The case became an example of the importance of following environmental regulations and maintaining proper safety procedures.

The project studies the following issues:

- Improper storage of hazardous chemicals.
- Leakage and damage to chemical containers.
- Inadequate labeling and monitoring.
- Insufficient ventilation and inspection.
- Failure to follow environmental safety requirements.
- Lack of effective corrective action.
- Professional negligence and accountability.

## 5. Ethical Issues Identified

The major ethical concerns discussed in this project include:

1. **Negligence:** Failure to take appropriate action despite unsafe conditions.
2. **Public Safety:** Lack of sufficient attention to risks affecting workers and surrounding communities.
3. **Environmental Responsibility:** Improper handling and disposal of hazardous materials.
4. **Professional Integrity:** Failure to maintain honesty, responsibility, and professional standards.
5. **Legal Compliance:** Failure to follow applicable environmental regulations.
6. **Accountability:** The responsibility of engineers and institutions for their professional decisions.
7. **Whistleblowing:** The importance of reporting unsafe or unethical practices through appropriate channels.

## 8. Solutions and Preventive Measures

The following measures are recommended to prevent similar incidents:

- Conduct regular inspections of chemical storage facilities.
- Use proper labeling and documentation systems.
- Maintain safe ventilation and storage conditions.
- Ensure responsible disposal of hazardous materials.
- Provide regular safety training to employees.
- Establish clear reporting procedures for safety violations.
- Protect employees who report genuine workplace concerns.
- Introduce independent monitoring and auditing systems.
- Encourage ethical decision-making at every level of an organization.
